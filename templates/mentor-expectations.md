# Mentor expectations

Thank you for mentoring in [Program name]. Your role is to help high school students become independent researchers. Here's what that involves.

## Before the summer
- [ ] Complete required background check and youth-protection training
- [ ] Read the project report students will receive
- [ ] Plan the techniques you'll teach and gather training materials
- [ ] Discuss these expectations at a lab meeting

## During the summer

### Teach toward independence
Use a gradual release:
1. **I do, you watch.** Demonstrate and explain.
2. **We do together.** Student does it with you alongside.
3. **You do, I watch.** Student leads; you observe and step in only if needed.
4. **You do.** Student works independently; you're available.

The goal is for students to run experiments on their own **by week three**.

### Ask before answering
When a student asks a question or an experiment fails, start with:
- "What do you think happened?"
- "What would you change next time?"
- "How could we find out?"

### Value their ideas
When a student proposes something, engage with it seriously. If it's feasible, help them try it. Being taken seriously is one of the most powerful parts of the program.

### Be present and predictable
- Check in daily: plan for the day, problems from yesterday.
- Tell students when you'll be away and who to go to instead.
- Review their notebooks and data regularly.

### Follow youth-protection rules
- Work in visible, shared spaces.
- Use approved group channels for communication, following [Institution]'s policies.
- Report any concerns to [contact].

## After the summer
- Help students write their part of any manuscript.
- Provide input for recommendation letters.
- Stay in touch through the cohort's channels.

## What you get
- Real mentoring experience for your CV, fellowships and job applications
- Co-authorship on publications you contribute to
- The chance to watch a student discover they're a scientist
