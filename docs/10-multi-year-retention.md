# 10 · Multi-year retention

The multi-year structure is the heart of LiNC. Students who return summer after summer build skills, confidence and relationships that a single summer cannot.

## Growing responsibility each year

| Summer | Typical grade completed | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 1** | 9th or 10th | **Learner.** Training, then first independent experiments and first budget |
| **Year 2** | 10th or 11th | **Researcher.** Faster start; designs more of the project; begins training new students |
| **Year 3** | 11th or 12th | **Leader.** Trains incoming students; leads parts of training; mentors through troubleshooting |
| **Year 4** (summer after graduation) | 12th | **Senior scholar.** Leads a sub-project; gives a capstone talk; prepares for college research (see [Beyond the summer](11-beyond-the-summer.md)) |

Returning students can begin independent work almost immediately, and they show measurable year-over-year gains in confidence and in their ability to teach others.

## Keeping students connected between summers

- **Fall:** involve students in writing the manuscript from their summer.
- **Winter:** send a personal invitation to return. Confirm before opening new spots.
- **Throughout the year:** a group message channel for the cohort (following your institution's rules for communicating with minors), an occasional virtual meeting, and congratulations on milestones.

## School-year weekend sessions

Local students can also come to the lab on **weekends during the school year**, if this is arranged through your institution's office for programs involving minors. The same paperwork, supervision and safety rules apply as in the summer, so set it up with that office before inviting students.

Weekend sessions help students:
- Finish experiments started over the summer
- Learn a new module ahead of next summer
- Stay connected to the lab and their cohort

Keep them optional, and schedule them around school, work and family commitments.

## When students can't return

Students' lives change: jobs, family obligations, other programs. Make it easy to return, and never penalize a student for missing a summer. Keep them on the mailing list and welcome them back if they can come in a later year.

## Measuring retention

Track for each student:
- Summers completed
- Whether they returned each eligible year, and if not, why
- Their role each summer

Report retention as **returns divided by eligible returns** (exclude students who graduated). Also report **student-summers**: the total number of summers completed across all students, which shows the depth of engagement.
