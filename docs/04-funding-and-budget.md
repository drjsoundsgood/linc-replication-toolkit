# 04 · Funding and budget

## What a summer costs

Costs depend heavily on location, whether the program is residential, and local wage norms. Use this as a checklist, then fill in your institution's numbers.

### Per student

| Category | Notes | Typical range (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| **Research budget** | Consumables the student plans and purchases for their own project. See [Student research budgets](08-student-budgets.md). | **$500–$1,000** for a biology project |
| **Stipend** | Removes the need for a competing summer job. Check your institution's rules for paying minors. | Set locally |
| **Housing and meals** | Residential programs only | Set by your housing office |
| **Local transit** | Commuting students | Cost of a monthly transit pass |
| **Lunch** | Shared lunches build community | Modest |
| **Lab coat, goggles, gloves** | Provide these; students keep the coat | Modest |

### Program-wide

| Category | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Site visit** | Travel to another university's lab for a day of talks and tours |
| **Final presentation event** | Refreshments; invite families |
| **Publication costs** | Open-access fees, if any |
| **Program office fees** | Some institutions charge for registration, insurance or administration |

## Where the money comes from

The founding program has never relied on a single source. It kept running through shifts in federal funding by **combining several types of support**. Aim for a similar mix.

| Source type | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal grants** | Supplements for high school students on existing awards; broader-impacts budgets in new proposals (for example NSF CAREER) | Check whether your current awards allow supplements for pre-college researchers |
| **Private donors** | Alumni, families, local foundations, industry partners | Multi-year commitments give stability. Work with your development office. |
| **Institutional funds** | Dean's office, department, diversity or outreach initiatives, pre-college program offices | Even a small annual commitment helps and signals institutional buy-in |
| **Discretionary funds** | Startup, endowed chair or professorship funds | Useful as a **backstop** to cover gaps between grants |
| **Society and foundation awards** | Scientific societies and education-focused foundations | Many fund teaching and outreach innovation |

### Tips
- **Write LiNC into your grants.** A concrete, multi-year program with measured outcomes is a strong broader-impacts plan.
- **Ask for multi-year commitments** from donors and deans, so you can promise students they can return.
- **Keep a reserve.** Identify a backstop source before you promise a student a second summer.
- **Report back to funders** with your survey results, publications and college outcomes. Good data makes renewal much easier.

## Scaling up or down

### Low-cost starting version
If funding is tight, run a smaller version rather than not running at all:
- 2 local students, working as a pair
- No housing; transit support only
- A smaller stipend
- A research budget at the lower end of the range
- One mentor plus the PI

This version still delivers the core experience: autonomy, a real project and the chance to return.

### Full version
- Up to 6 students per summer, working in pairs or trios
- Local and out-of-town students, all housed together to build community
- A full stipend
- A research budget at the higher end of the range
- A site visit to another institution

### Growth path
Start small, collect data from year one, and use it to fund expansion. The founding program grew from 3 students in its first summer to 6, and from 3 applicants to more than 50.
