# 05 · Recruitment and selection

## Who LiNC is for

LiNC is **open to all students**. The aim is **inclusive excellence**: excellent science comes from a community that draws on talent from everywhere, and every participant, whatever their background, helps build that community.

- **Eligibility:** high school students finishing 9th or 10th grade, so they can return for multiple summers. That's the only requirement.
- **No prior research experience required.** Curiosity and commitment matter more than credentials.
- **Outreach goes where opportunity is scarce.** Recruit actively at under-resourced schools and through community organizations, and reach students who have little access to research, such as students from low-income families and first-generation college-bound students.
- **Selection rewards a commitment to inclusion.** Applicants respond to scenarios about making science welcoming to others. You don't need to belong to a particular group to help others feel they belong in science.

Recruiting early is deliberate. Students who join after 9th grade can complete three or four summers, and the program reaches them before many have decided science "isn't for people like me."

Check with your institution's program office and general counsel on how to describe and apply eligibility and selection criteria. Rules vary by institution and funder and have been changing.

## Where to find students

Cast a wide net. The students who would benefit most are often the least likely to hear about research opportunities.

| Channel | How |
|---|---|
| **Your institution's pre-college programs page** | List the program each year. This was a major source of applicants for the founding program. |
| **Local public high schools** | Email science teachers and guidance counselors directly, with a one-page flyer. Teachers are your best advocates. |
| **Community organizations** | Youth programs, libraries, community centers, faith organizations, college-access nonprofits |
| **Existing outreach programs** | Partner with programs that already serve the students you want to reach |
| **Inbound emails** | Students who cold-email you are showing initiative. Point them to the application. |
| **Current and former students** | Word of mouth from LiNC participants and their families is powerful once the program is running |

Make the flyer and application accessible: plain language, mobile-friendly forms, the stipend stated up front, and a clear statement that **no experience is needed**.

## The application

Keep it short enough that a busy student can finish it in under an hour. The founding program's application asks for:

1. **Basic information:** name, grade, school, contact details, guardian contact.
2. **Essay: Why are you interested in science?** What sparked the interest? What questions do you wonder about?
3. **Essay: What do you want to be when you grow up?** There's no wrong answer. This shows how students see their future.
4. **Scenario questions on inclusion in science.** Short responses describing how the student would handle situations involving inclusion and belonging, for example welcoming someone new to a team, or responding when someone's idea is dismissed. These show a student's values and how they'll contribute to the lab community.
5. **Availability:** confirmation the student can commit 20 hours per week for five weeks, and whether they could return in future summers.
6. **Optional:** a short teacher recommendation or a few sentences from a mentor.

A ready-to-copy version is in [templates/application-form.md](../templates/application-form.md).

**Avoid** requiring grades, test scores or prior research. These favor students who already have advantages and tell you little about how someone will do in a lab.

## Selecting students

Use a rubric so decisions are consistent and defensible. See [templates/selection-rubric.md](../templates/selection-rubric.md). The rubric scores:

- **Curiosity:** genuine interest in how the living world works
- **Commitment:** availability and motivation to return in future summers
- **Commitment to inclusion:** thoughtful responses to the inclusion scenarios, showing how the student would help others belong
- **Opportunity:** how much the program would add, given the student's current access to research opportunities

Then:

1. Have **two reviewers** score each application independently, for example the PI and a senior mentor.
2. Invite finalists to a **15-minute video conversation**. Keep it friendly: it's a chance for the student to ask questions too.
3. Build a cohort that **balances** grade levels (so someone can return each year) and brings together students who will learn from each other.
4. Send offers with a clear response deadline, and keep a **waitlist**.

## Balancing new and returning students

Once the program is established, most spots in a given summer may go to returning students. Decide each winter how many new spots you have. A good steady state for a six-student program is **two new students per year**, entering as a pair, so the cohort always mixes new and returning students.
