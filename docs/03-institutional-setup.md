# 03 · Institutional setup

Hosting minors in a research lab involves legal, safety and logistical requirements. **You should not handle these alone.** Most research universities have an office that runs programs involving minors. It may be called a pre-college programs office, a youth programs office, an office of sponsored programs for minors, or similar. Find it first.

In the founding program, this office handled **all of the paperwork**, including family consent forms and research agreements, plus registration and much of the logistics. The PI's role was to design the program, review applications and run the science.

## Step 1: Find your partners

| Partner | What they typically handle |
|---|---|
| **Office for programs involving minors** | Program registration, applications portal, family consent forms, research participation agreements, medical forms, emergency contacts, insurance, youth-protection policies |
| **Environmental health and safety (EHS)** | Which lab areas and hazards minors may access; required safety training |
| **Housing / residential life** (if residential) | Dorm rooms, supervision, meals, weekend activities |
| **Human resources or payroll** | How students receive stipends (stipend, wage, or scholarship) |
| **Sponsored research / grants office** | Allowable costs on your grants; donor gift accounts |
| **Department administrator** | Purchasing for student budgets; room bookings; travel |

## Step 2: Learn the requirements

Ask your institution about each of these. Requirements vary widely.

### Youth protection
- [ ] Background checks for every adult who will work with students
- [ ] Youth-protection training for all mentors (often online, 1–2 hours)
- [ ] Rules on one-on-one contact (many institutions require two adults or an open-door, visible setting)
- [ ] Rules on electronic communication with minors (often: group channels, copy a second adult or parent)
- [ ] Reporting procedures for concerns

### Lab safety
- [ ] Minimum age for lab access, and whether parental consent is needed
- [ ] Required safety training before entering the lab
- [ ] Which hazards are off-limits (commonly radioactivity, certain biological agents, highly toxic chemicals, some equipment)
- [ ] Personal protective equipment (provide it; students should not have to buy it)
- [ ] Supervision ratio in the lab

Design projects around what students **can** safely do. Many molecular biology techniques, including RNA synthesis, gel electrophoresis, binding assays and spectroscopy, are suitable with training and supervision.

### Documents families sign
Your program office typically provides these:
- [ ] Program participation agreement
- [ ] Research participation agreement (time commitment, conduct, confidentiality, data ownership)
- [ ] Medical information and emergency contacts
- [ ] Media release (optional; keep it separate so students can decline)
- [ ] Code of conduct

### Time commitment
The founding program asks students to commit **20 hours per week** for five weeks. State the commitment clearly in the application and the participation agreement so families can plan around jobs and family responsibilities.

### Compensation
Students should be paid. A stipend removes a major barrier for students who would otherwise need a summer job. Ask HR or the program office how stipends to minors are processed at your institution, and whether they are taxable.

## Step 3: Register the program

Many institutions require programs with minors to be formally registered each year. Registration often gives you:

- An application portal
- Insurance coverage for participants
- Access to housing and dining
- A listing on the institution's pre-college programs website, which is a strong recruitment channel

## Step 4: Residential or commuting?

| | Commuting (local students) | Residential |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Lower: stipend, research budget, transit, lunch | Higher: adds housing, meals, supervision, travel |
| **Reach** | Students within commuting distance | Students from anywhere |
| **Community** | Good | Stronger: shared evenings and weekends build a cohort |
| **Complexity** | Lower | Higher: evening and weekend supervision |

A good path is to **start commuting-only**, then add housing once funding allows.
