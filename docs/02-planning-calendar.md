# 02 · Planning calendar

This calendar assumes a five-week program running in **July and early August**. Shift everything if your summer falls differently. The first year takes the most lead time; after that the cycle repeats with much less effort.

## Year one: 12 months out

### 12–9 months before (summer–fall of the prior year)
- [ ] Decide on scope: number of students (start with 2–4), local-only or residential, dates.
- [ ] Meet your institution's **office for programs involving minors** (sometimes called youth programs, pre-college programs, or sponsored programs for minors). Learn what they handle and what they require. See [Institutional setup](03-institutional-setup.md).
- [ ] Draft your module set and assign a mentor pair to each module (see [templates/module-catalog.md](../templates/module-catalog.md)). Confirm summer availability.
- [ ] Draft a budget and identify funding. See [Funding and budget](04-funding-and-budget.md).
- [ ] Choose the research questions students will tackle. See [Connecting a course to the summer](06-course-to-lab-link.md).

### 9–6 months before (fall–winter)
- [ ] Apply for funding (grant supplements, institutional funds, donors).
- [ ] Register the program with your institution if required.
- [ ] Finalize the application form and selection rubric (see [`templates/`](../templates/)).
- [ ] Contact local high schools, community organizations and teachers.

### 6–4 months before (January–March)
- [ ] **Open applications.** Keep them open 6–8 weeks.
- [ ] Post on your institution's pre-college program website, if it has one.
- [ ] Teach the spring course or run the spring computational work that generates student projects.

### 4–3 months before (March–April)
- [ ] Close applications and review them with the rubric.
- [ ] Interview finalists (short video calls work well).
- [ ] Send offers. Have a waitlist ready.

### 3–1 months before (April–June)
- [ ] Your institution's program office collects family consent, research agreements, medical and insurance forms.
- [ ] Complete mentor background checks and youth-protection training.
- [ ] Prepare accessible project reports for students to read (see [templates/accessible-report-template.md](../templates/accessible-report-template.md)).
- [ ] Order shared training supplies; set up student budget trackers.
- [ ] Plan the site visit to another university's lab.
- [ ] Send students their pre-reading and welcome letter.
- [ ] Prepare the **pre-program survey**.

### The summer (5 weeks)
See [The five-week summer](07-summer-curriculum.md). Administer the pre-program survey on day one and the post-program survey in the final week.

### After the summer (August–December)
- [ ] Analyze survey data.
- [ ] Begin manuscript writing with students. See [Publishing](12-publishing.md).
- [ ] Invite eligible students to return. See [Multi-year retention](10-multi-year-retention.md).
- [ ] Support graduating seniors through college applications. See [Beyond the summer](11-beyond-the-summer.md).
- [ ] Debrief with mentors: what to keep, what to change.

## Steady state: the annual cycle

Once running, the year looks like this:

| Season | Course / lab | LiNC |
|---|---|---|
| **Fall** | Plan next year's projects | Manuscript writing; college application support for seniors; alumni check-ins |
| **Winter** | | Funding renewals; open applications for new students; confirm returning students |
| **Spring** | Course or lab work generates testable predictions and accessible reports | Select new students; paperwork; mentor training |
| **Summer** | | Five-week program; pre/post surveys; site visit; final presentations |

## Weekly time commitment for the PI

| Period | Approximate PI hours per week |
|---|---|
| Recruitment and selection | 2–4 |
| Program preparation (spring) | 1–2 |
| During the summer | 5–10 (meetings, check-ins, presentations) |
| Fall (manuscripts, college support) | 1–2 |

Day-to-day supervision during the summer is carried by the mentorship cascade, not the PI alone. See [The mentorship cascade](09-mentorship-cascade.md).
