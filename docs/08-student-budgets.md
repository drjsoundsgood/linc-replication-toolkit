# 08 · Student research budgets

Each LiNC student manages their own **research budget of $500–$1,000** for a biology project. They decide what to buy, justify it, place orders (with help) and track spending.

## Why give high schoolers a budget?

- **Ownership.** It's their experiment, paid from their budget.
- **Care.** Students who know what a reagent costs are more careful with it, and more meticulous in how they plan and run experiments.
- **Financial literacy.** Students learn to plan, prioritize and track spending, skills that transfer far beyond the lab.
- **Demystifying research.** Students see how science is actually resourced, which is part of academia's unwritten curriculum.

## How it works

### 1. Shared versus personal costs
Decide in advance what comes out of the student's budget and what the lab covers.

| Lab covers | Student budget covers |
|---|---|
| Safety equipment and PPE | Project-specific reagents (for example primers, oligonucleotides, enzymes, kits) |
| Training supplies used in weeks 1–2 | Consumables for their experiments |
| Shared stock solutions and common plastics | Any re-orders after a failed experiment |
| Equipment use | |

### 2. Plan (week 2)
Students complete a **budget proposal** ([templates/budget-proposal.md](../templates/budget-proposal.md)) listing:
- The question they're testing
- Each item, the supplier, catalog number, quantity and price
- Why each item is needed
- A reserve of about 15–20% for repeats and surprises

### 3. Approve (start of week 3)
A mentor reviews the proposal with the student and asks questions:
- Is there a cheaper option that works as well?
- Does the lab already have this?
- What happens if the first attempt fails?

The PI gives final approval.

### 4. Order
The student prepares the order details; the lab manager or department administrator places the order through the institution's purchasing system. Students should never use their own money or personal cards.

### 5. Track
Students record every purchase in a tracker ([templates/budget-tracker.csv](../templates/budget-tracker.csv)): what was ordered, when it arrived, what it cost and what's left. They report their budget status at subgroup meetings.

### 6. Reflect
In their final presentation, students briefly explain how they used their budget and what they would do differently.

## Tips

- **Account for shipping times.** Order early in week 3; some reagents take a week or more to arrive.
- **Pool where it makes sense.** Pairs testing related predictions can share some items, with costs split.
- **Unspent funds** return to the program to support the next cohort. Say so up front; it encourages thrift without discouraging necessary purchases.
